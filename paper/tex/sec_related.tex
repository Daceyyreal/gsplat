\section{Related work}
\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{Compressing 3D Gaussian Splatting.}
3D Gaussian Splatting represents a scene by millions of anisotropic Gaussians, each with a position, a covariance, an opacity and a view-dependent colour stored as spherical-harmonic (SH) coefficients, which make up most of each Gaussian's stored floats \citep{kerbl2023gaussian}.
Bagdasarian et al.\ and Ali et al.\ survey the compression methods for it \citep{bagdasarian2025zip,ali2026compression}, and Splatwizard and Yang et al.\ benchmark them, the latter with a subjective quality study \citep{liu2026splatwizard,yang2024benchmark}.
Some learn the compact representation while the scene is trained: a mask that removes Gaussians, a grid-based neural field in place of SH colour and codebooks for the geometry \citep{lee2024compact}, or $k$-means vector quantisation (VQ) of the parameters while they are optimised \citep{navaneet2024compgs}.
NSVQ-GS trains separate attribute codebooks jointly with the model through noise-substituted vector quantisation \citep{wang2025compressing}.
Others store quantised embeddings \citep{girish2024eagles} or entropy-code anchor attributes with a learned context model, a hash grid in HAC and HAC++ and an anchor-level autoregressive model in ContextGS \citep{chen2024hac,chen2025hacpp,wang2024contextgs}; these learn the representation while the scene is trained, and this paper does not compare with them.
Post-training methods start from a trained model: LightGaussian prunes Gaussians by their global significance with a recovery step, distils the SH colour to a lower degree and vector-quantises it \citep{fan2024lightgaussian}.
Papantonakis et al.\ combine resolution-aware pruning, a per-Gaussian choice of SH degree, codebook quantisation and half-precision floats \citep{papantonakis2024reducing}.
MesonGS prunes by a view-dependent and view-independent importance, transforms the attributes to lower their entropy and fine-tunes to restore quality \citep{xie2024mesongs}, and MesonGS++ chooses its pruning ratio and bit widths under a size budget without training \citep{xie2026mesongspp}.
POTR prunes by each splat's removal effect and recomputes the lighting coefficients without training, with an optional fine-tuning step \citep{ramlot2026potr}.
GSICO arranges a trained model's parameters into images for a conventional image codec \citep{martin2026structured}, KISS-GS separates compression from training and makes fine-tuning optional \citep{morgenstern2026kiss}, and 3DTurboQuant replaces a learned codebook with a random rotation and data-independent Lloyd--Max quantisation \citep{lee2026turboquant}.
This paper works in the post-training setting, and its host is C3DGS \citep{niedermayr2024compressed}.

C3DGS scores each colour and shape parameter by its sensitivity, the magnitude of the gradient of the rendered training images' total energy (the sum of their pixel values) with respect to it, accumulated over the training views, and gives each Gaussian the largest sensitivity among its colour parameters and, separately, among its shape parameters \citep{niedermayr2024compressed}. % C3DGS paper Sec. 4.1, Eq. 3, App. E; code at 2a234af5: compress.py:40-84, 154-156
It removes the Gaussians whose colour sensitivity is zero, clusters the colours of the others into a codebook with sensitivity-weighted $k$-means, and lets the Gaussians whose colour sensitivity lies above a threshold keep their own colour \citep{niedermayr2024compressed}. % Sec. 4.1, Eqs. 5-6, App. E; compression/vq.py:37-60, 78-117, 155, 250-253; arguments/__init__.py:75, 92
It clusters the shape in the same way, as normalised covariance matrices with a separate scale factor per Gaussian, quantises every parameter except the position to 8 bits and stores positions as 16-bit floats, fine-tunes the model with the quantisation in the loop,\footnote{C3DGS's paper says its fine-tuning optimises each Gaussian's position, opacity and scaling factor and the two codebooks \citep[Sec.~4.2]{niedermayr2024compressed}. In its released code at \code{2a234af5}, which this paper ran, the position's learning rate is its initial value times \code{spatial\_lr\_scale} (\code{scene/gaussian\_model.py:231} and \code{scene/gaussian\_model.py:268-269}), and \code{spatial\_lr\_scale} is \code{0} (\code{scene/gaussian\_model.py:69}) unless a training checkpoint is restored (\code{scene/gaussian\_model.py:136}, \code{compress.py:135-137}), which these runs never do, so positions do not move during fine-tuning.} and stores the arrays, sorted in Morton order, with DEFLATE compression \citep{niedermayr2024compressed}. % Sec. 4.1, App. F, Sec. 4.2, Sec. 4.3; compression/vq.py:194-238; scene/gaussian_model.py:78-88, 182-198, 450-451, 463, 575; compress.py:231; arguments/__init__.py:72, 90; finetune.py:12-24
This paper changes only C3DGS's colour codebook and keeps the rest of its pipeline as released, fine-tuning included (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). % Am17 b

\paragraph{Observation-Gram compaction.}
OGC belongs to a group of 2026 works that weight or restrict SH colour by how each Gaussian is observed.
OGC \citep{pietroszek2026only} weights a Gaussian's SH colour by what the training cameras saw.
For each Gaussian it accumulates a $16\times16$ observation Gram matrix over the SH basis: a sum over the training views of the outer product of the SH basis at the viewing direction, weighted by the Gaussian's compositing weights in that view, so that a colour error in a direction no training camera observed costs nothing \citep{pietroszek2026only}.
In its own pipeline OGC uses the matrix to project the colour onto fewer SH degrees, to allocate coefficients among Gaussians, and as the metric of a generalised Lloyd VQ of the AC coefficients \citep{pietroszek2026only}.
Hosted in C3DGS, it replaces only C3DGS's colour VQ with that Gram-metric Lloyd VQ on all 16 SH rows, keeps C3DGS's pruning, shape VQ, fine-tuning and container, and reports a paired PSNR gain over its reproduction of C3DGS of +0.49 dB before C3DGS's fine-tuning and +0.09 dB after it, on nine Mip-NeRF 360 scenes \citep{barron2022mipnerf360} \citep[Table~1 and Sec.~4.1]{pietroszek2026only}. % OGC's Table 1, row "our matrix A_i in generalised Lloyd" before and after fine-tuning, and its Sec. 4.1; A4, A5
Its paper states a ridge of $10^{-6}$ in the VQ's codeword update, while its released code defaults to $10^{-3}$; this paper uses the code's value (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). % RELATED_WORK_OGC.md 2 b; Am17 b; B7; CLAIMS Gap 12
This paper replicates OGC's host comparison under a pre-registered protocol (Section~\ref{sec:replication}), tests whether the lead survives fine-tuning at equal rate (Section~\ref{sec:finetuning}), and dissects which parts of OGC's VQ carry the lead (Section~\ref{sec:dissection}). % Am17; Am19
This bears on the training-free codecs above: before C3DGS's fine-tuning OGC's VQ led by 0.3558~dB at equal rate and after it by 0.0425~dB, which suggests that the metric matters most where no fine-tuning follows; no training-free codec was tested here. % s19 "The verdict"; s21 "The verdict"; A2, F1

Do, Chou and Cheung \citep{do2026transforming} also weight SH coefficients by how they are observed, but with one global Gram matrix over all basis functions of all splats, approximated as a product of spatial, directional and colour factors.
Its square root orthonormalises the coefficients before uniform scalar quantisation and entropy coding; the method codes sparse voxel splats rather than 3DGS models, and has no VQ \citep{do2026transforming}.
Zhou restricts each Gaussian's AC coefficients during training to the subspace that the local Gram structure of its observations supports \citep{zhou2026efficient}, and Lieber et al.\ map image information back onto splats by solving a visibility-weighted least-squares problem per Gaussian through its normal equations, for relighting, stylisation and segmentation rather than compression \citep{lieber2026instant}.

\paragraph{Second-order and observation-weighted quantisation.}
Weighting a quantiser's error by a second-order model of the loss predates both methods.
In neural-network compression the Hessian of the loss, or its Gauss--Newton approximation, has been used to choose which weights to prune \citep{lecun1989optimal,hassibi1992second}, to weight the $k$-means clustering of weights \citep{choi2017towards}, to set per-layer bit widths \citep{dong2019hawq}, and to quantise weights one at a time while the remaining weights compensate \citep{frantar2022optimal,frantar2023optq}.
In 3D Gaussian Splatting, PUP 3D-GS prunes Gaussians by a second-order approximation of the training-view reconstruction error with respect to each Gaussian's spatial parameters \citep{hanson2025pup}, and Van hauwermeiren et al.\ quantise the attributes with a weighted Lloyd--Max quantiser that minimises an importance-weighted squared error \citep{vanhauwermeiren2026nonuniform}. % Van hauwermeiren et al.: abstract and Sec. 3.2
Layer-wise quantisers that minimise the error of a layer's outputs on calibration data weight the error by the Gram matrix of the layer's inputs, which is the same construction applied to activations \citep{nagel2020up,frantar2023optq}.
For one Gaussian the rendered colour is linear in its SH coefficients, ignoring the clamp of negative colours, so a Gauss--Newton metric of the image error with respect to those coefficients has the form of OGC's observation Gram; the metric used in this paper is a 16-probe estimate of it, equal to OGC's Gram in expectation by construction (Section~\ref{sec:setup}). % Am17 b; A12; RELATED_WORK_OGC.md 2 a, 3
C3DGS's sensitivity is a first-order, scalar counterpart: one weight per Gaussian, the largest gradient magnitude among its colour parameters, so its clustering distance does not distinguish viewing directions. % C3DGS paper Sec. 4.1, Eq. 5; compress.py:154; compression/vq.py:37-60
OGC's scalar variant, the trace of the Gram matrix times the identity, lies between the two, and Section~\ref{sec:gram-trace} tests the full matrix against it. % Am17 b; B1
This project began with a Gauss--Newton-weighted VQ of the SH codebook in gsplat's PNG codec (GN-VQ), which was never compared with OGC on a gate scene and was retired before the replication (Section~\ref{sec:gnvq}). % s17; Am17 a; C5, D6
